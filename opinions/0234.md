# BRC-234: Progressive Contraction of Free Chain-Scanned Index Coverage

Ty Everett (ty@projectbabbage.com)

> **Status:** Proposed specification, version 1. This is a voluntary contract for free TXID, address and script-hash query coverage. It does not assert universal adoption, provider availability, measured savings, or deployment of every future transport discussed below. Research status: 2026-10-20.

## Abstract

This BRC proposes coordinated contraction of the keyspace for which participating BSV mainnet indexers promise free access. It covers generalized TXID retrieval and address/script-hash history, output-list and balance queries. Block `H0 = 1,000,000` anchors 27 contractions, each requiring both calendar and cumulative transaction-volume gates. Every actual contraction additionally has a deterministic, stage-specific 14-day notice round. The final calendar gate is 78 months after the anchor; notice, block arrival and insufficient volume can delay activation.

Address or script-hash coverage requires the corresponding key filter **and** the funding transaction's TXID filter. TXID lookup requires only the TXID filter, regardless of contained scripts or addresses. Script-hash coverage uses `HASH160(lockingScript)` and a growing leading-bit constraint toward repeated hexadecimal `7`, with expected uniform-hash grinding work between the address work and twice that work. The terminal address and TXID prefixes are `1ChickenNugget` and thirteen `9`s.

Stages 0–12 require complete confirmed-spend accounting for admitted outputs. From stage 13, only spends whose TXIDs meet their event-stage rule must be reflected. Returned outputs and amounts can consequently be stale relative to the real UTXO set; providers MUST document this reduced guarantee. Existing APIs and delivery formats can remain unchanged. There is no whole-address or whole-script-hash disqualification mechanism. Previously admitted outputs remain grandfathered across later funding-TXID contractions while their query key remains eligible.

Providers may declare caps on free retrieval/history coverage, but an offered output/balance ledger must still account for every admitted output while its query key is eligible. Conformance is defined by those availability and spend-accounting guarantees, independently of API or delivery format.

Accepted-chain history alone governs stage and ledger state; reorganizations undo orphaned notice rounds, activations, admissions and spend effects. Providers SHOULD retain recovery data for at least 100 accepted descendants before destructive removal, disclose their actual policy, and return unavailable during any unreconstructed gap. No provision requires purging data or prohibits a shared complete backing index, a paid archive, or paid access to broader keyspaces.

## Motivation and rationale

### The reliability contract being retired

The primary concern is the expectation that comprehensive indexing will remain available free of charge, indefinitely, as part of an application's reliability and recovery contract. Historical transaction lookup entries accumulate as transactions accumulate. Address and script indexes add output records, spend state, query work, replication, bandwidth and operational recovery costs. Transaction growth can accelerate these burdens; this proposal does not claim that every index's cost follows a proven exponential curve. A free API does not make its operation costless: an operator, sponsor, other customer or another participant still bears the bill.

The author considers that arrangement increasingly misaligned with a growing BSV blockchain. An application should not externalize indefinite retention, discovery and recovery obligations onto parties that receive no corresponding revenue. A private key supplies signing authority; it does not supply the outpoints, transaction bodies, derivation instructions, current spend state or validation evidence needed to use that authority. A seed alone is not an entitlement to free rediscovery of all those records at arbitrary future scale.

A secondary concern is completeness trust. An index can omit payments or spends, lag, or present incomplete balances. An inclusion proof can authenticate a returned transaction but does not prove that an address's history is complete or that an output remains unspent. Multiple independent providers can improve resilience, but agreement is not a cryptographic proof against correlated omission. Direct delivery and locally validated evidence reduce the need to discover one's transactions through that intermediary; they do not abolish chain, availability or conflict-detection dependencies.

### Why a shared contraction profile

Independent, incompatible filters could leave applications unable to use the same keys across providers. A common sequence of nested keyspaces preserves a shared eligible keyspace while free guarantees shrink. The accepted chain determines the same notice rounds and stages for every conforming provider at the same tip. Operators still have to provide complete data for their declared scope and snapshot; a common filter alone does not establish correctness, capacity or availability. Permitted provider-specific retrieval/history caps can limit the actual overlap of available records, so the shared keyspace does not promise identical archives. Optional balance services retain the complete canonical admitted ledger independently of those retrieval caps.

The policy deliberately favors migration to direct delivery, peer-to-peer transaction exchange and purpose-built overlays. The author's architectural position is that use cases can be designed around those methods without requiring a universal free index. This is a design position, not a claim that every application has completed its migration or that every proposed infrastructure component is deployed. Paid indexing is an expressly legitimate alternative, including for explorers, exchanges, researchers, auditors and historical recovery.

A provider may offer current stage `n` free, stage `n-1` for a fee, a broader stage `n-m` for another fee, or the whole keyspace commercially. Revenue may subsidize the free portion. This BRC sets no prices and regulates no internal storage layout: one complete backing index may serve all of these products. Success includes the continued existence of sustainable comprehensive paid services. The desired outcome is the end of reliance on an unpriced, universal, perpetual fallback, with explicit responsibility for preserving and paying for necessary data.

Charging for comprehensive access could itself fund indexing. Sponsorship and cross-subsidy can also support broader free access; the author does not consider a zero-price product intrinsically unsustainable or illegitimate. The objection is relying indefinitely on coverage for which continued funding and responsibility have not been secured. A willing provider may continue broader complimentary service under its own availability commitment.

The reason for this particular transition is to preserve a commonly eligible public keyspace while gradually increasing the cost of relying on it. A price-only change could fund a service, but does not by itself define a common remaining free pool. Provider-specific quotas can constrain demand, but do not by themselves coordinate which keys remain eligible across providers. The filters can be evaluated without per-key registration, allocation or a common customer database, and the TXID constraint also supports selective downstream propagation. Providers can combine this profile with paid tiers, declared history caps and ordinary access limits. These are policy tradeoffs, not evidence that prefix grinding is the only sustainable solution or an economically optimal one.

Grinding consumes participant resources without paying the index provider. The author accepts that disadvantage as part of a gradual migration pressure, rather than proposing it as a more efficient substitute for paying for useful services. The profile aims to reduce the free service's required dataset and, after stage 12, its spend-observation scope. It guarantees neither a fixed operating budget nor a hard traffic bound: qualifying traffic can still grow, users can batch many outputs into a qualifying transaction, and live grandfathered ledgers still require state. The cost and migration rationale should be assessed with those limits intact.

### Evidence and its limits

The operational burden is concrete. BSV's [SV Node storage guidance](https://docs.bsvblockchain.org/network-topology/nodes/sv-node/installation/sv-node/aws-volumes-setup) described approximately 12 TB of block data and 350 GB of chainstate in January 2024, with performance-sensitive UTXO storage distinct from cheaper block storage. These are dated node-storage figures, not a current measurement of an explorer index or a forecast of this proposal's savings. The association's [May 2024 report](https://resources.bsvblockchain.org/hubfs/BSV%20report/BSV%20blockchain%20digital%20transformation%20-%20The%20Role%20of%20Blockchain%20in%20Safeguarding%20Data%20and%20Streamlining%20Transactions.pdf) reported over 3.6 billion processed transactions. Historical records grow cumulatively; UTXO-set size can rise or fall with output creation and spending and MUST NOT be presented as monotonically growing merely because history grows.

Blockchair's [June 2022 operator account](https://github.com/Blockchair/Blockchair.Support/issues/910#issuecomment-1159369293) described large-block transfer, duplicated explorer databases, disk/development costs and too little BSV API demand to cover them. Its [later support response](https://github.com/Blockchair/Blockchair.Support/issues/1045#issuecomment-1504229868) confirmed limiting full public BSV support on 2022-11-07. The same incident included node-version and database problems; it is evidence of that operator's experience, not proof of a universal technical ceiling. The author's economic inference is that unpaid comprehensive coverage is an unreliable long-term application dependency.

Exchange support can also end, but causation must be attributed honestly. For example, [Kraken's 2019 announcement](https://blog.kraken.com/product/kraken-is-delisting-bsv) gave conduct-related reasons. This BRC does not claim that exchange delistings generally establish index-cost causation. For exchanges choosing to support BSV, receiving relevant transactions and proofs directly can reduce the need for full-history discovery; exchange-specific security, accounting, liquidity and operating requirements remain their responsibility.

No measured global cost saving or statistically calibrated optimum is asserted. The author proposes the anchor, six-month initial runway, 78-month final calendar gate, 10% interval growth and volume milestones as reasonable starting policy judgments. Future proposals may argue for alternatives. Raw transaction count directly relates to the number of TXID lookup entries in a complete transaction index, although byte cost, output count, query load and hardware also matter. Volume is a scale gate, not proof of migration readiness; generated traffic counts too and cannot advance the calendar gates.

### Intended end state and tradeoffs

Early stages provide time to prepare before extreme grinding becomes necessary. Later grinding is intentionally prohibitive for ordinary legacy use. Its cost is borne by the party insisting on continued free-index eligibility; the author's expectation is that direct delivery or paid access will become preferable. This is not a promise of a particular future service price, key-search throughput or transaction-generation cost. Grinding is a transition pressure, not a resource-saving computation for its own sake, and no hard upper bound on admitted traffic is claimed.

The fixed final filters remain part of version 1. The intended economic outcome is that essentially no ordinary use remains, allowing operators eventually to close the free legacy product or offer remaining users paid service. Actual closure is a separate service decision with notice; this version has no mandatory shutdown stage. Providers do not promise to operate forever merely by adopting it.

The terminal strings are arbitrary and carry no cryptographic privilege. Repeated `9`s and `7`s are recognizable; Chicken Nugget is the name of the author's sister's kitten. The author states that no qualifying infrastructure was prepared in advance. The strings are public predicates anyone may target, not registrations or allocations to chosen participants.

## Specification

**MUST**, **MUST NOT**, **SHOULD** and **MAY** apply to services voluntarily claiming conformance. A provider MUST identify which free index families and optional output/balance operations it offers. **Conformance means availability of the required data and fulfillment of the defined spend-accounting guarantees.** It does not standardize API semantics, wire formats, delivery, ownership, consensus validity, custody, internal databases or commercial pricing. Providers may retain their existing interfaces; no new endpoint, method, parameter, response field, header, error code, pagination format or client rewrite is required by this BRC. Named variables and logical classifications below describe the coverage calculation and guarantees, not a required API schema.

`Free` means the advertised profile scope is obtainable without paying for that query or purchasing a broader indexing entitlement. Providers MAY require authentication and publish reasonable access, rate and resource limits; they MUST disclose them and MUST NOT use them to conceal that the claimed free service is effectively absent. Declared caps on TXID retrieval and address/script-hash history are permitted under section 6; they do not cap admitted output/balance ledgers under section 7. Paid priority, bulk delivery and broader coverage remain separate offers. This profile is not a promise of unlimited free bandwidth, arbitrary historical proof work or an availability SLA.

### 1. Canonical keys and independent query families

The profile applies to BSV mainnet. `MTP(B)` is the median Unix timestamp of accepted block `B` and its ten immediate ancestors. `T0 = MTP(H0)` is the UTC anchor. No publication date or forecast supplies that value. Before the accepted chain reaches `H0`, there is no activated profile and historical admission uses unrestricted stage 0.

The canonical representations below make the mathematical predicates unambiguous. They do not change an existing service's accepted request encodings or returned formats: providers may normalize or map their native identifiers internally before evaluating coverage.

A P2PKH address MUST be a canonical, checksum-valid mainnet Base58Check encoding of version byte `0x00` and a 20-byte public-key HASH160. Its output script is exactly `76a914{20-byte public-key hash}88ac`. The checksum is the first four bytes of double-SHA256 of version plus payload. Validate decoded length, version, checksum and canonical re-encoding. Do not fold case or trim whitespace. Other script templates are outside the address family but may use the script-hash family.

The script coverage key is `RIPEMD160(SHA256(S))`, where `S` is the exact serialized locking-script byte string. This is the hash of the script, not a public-key hash extracted from it. For coverage calculations its canonical display is 40 hexadecimal characters in digest-byte order, most significant bit first. The canonical filter notation uses no byte reversal, `0x`, padding, truncation or whitespace. This does not require an API to accept or return this 20-byte identifier. All locking-script templates may use this family; hashing an arbitrary string is not evidence that its script can be spent.

Existing native script identifiers remain unchanged. Providers MUST apply the coverage predicate to the corresponding script's coverage key, rather than apply the hexadecimal-`7` rule directly to an unrelated native identifier. For a native identifier `h = SHA256(S)`, the coverage key is exactly `RIPEMD160(h)`, computed over the 32 digest bytes, not their hexadecimal text. A reversed-SHA256 representation is first restored to digest-byte order. These identities need neither a new query parameter nor a script preimage supplied by the caller. Other identifiers may use an established internal script mapping; a digest cannot simply be truncated or relabeled. Providers MUST establish the script/key relation for data claimed within coverage and document their mapping, without having to add scripts or coverage keys to existing responses. Hash equality alone does not establish that a caller controls a script. No particular provider's identifier format defines conformance.

For coverage calculations, a TXID's canonical display is exactly 64 lowercase hexadecimal characters without whitespace or `0x`. Apply leading-bit tests to conventional big-endian display order, not to a little-endian internal buffer. Native representations may be mapped to this form internally.

```text
leadingBitsMatch(validatedHex, repeatedDigit, requiredBits):
  t = lowercase(validatedHex)
  whole = requiredBits // 4
  partial = requiredBits % 4
  if t[0:whole] != repeatedDigit repeated whole times: return false
  if partial == 0: return true
  return (hexValue(t[whole]) >> (4-partial)) ==
         (hexValue(repeatedDigit) >> (4-partial))

txidMatches(txid, stage) = leadingBitsMatch(txid, "9", TXID_BITS[stage])
scriptHashMatches(h, stage) = leadingBitsMatch(h, "7", SCRIPT_BITS[stage])
```

Address and script-hash eligibility are independent alternatives: neither family additionally requires the other's key predicate. Each additionally requires the funding TXID predicate for generalized history/admission. TXID eligibility is independent of both key families. Raw transaction retrieval returns the complete transaction, never redacted addresses or scripts. A provider supporting only one family MUST NOT imply that it offers the others.

The script-hash family includes generalized public lookups exposed through raw-script queries or alternative script identifiers; changing the API key representation does not exempt equivalent script-based discovery. Private wallet records, topical overlays and separately offered broader services retain their own contracts. This BRC does not contract the wallet's own authenticated list of transactions it received directly.

Let `N(s)` be the exact number of 20-byte public-key HASH160 values whose mainnet Base58Check addresses satisfy stage `s`. `SCRIPT_BITS[s]` is the smallest nonnegative integer `b` for which `N(s) * 2^b >= 2^160`. The resulting script-hash mean search work is never less than the address mean and is less than twice as large, under uniform-hash models. The first 17 contractions refine the address to `1Chi` and the TXID to `999`; subsequent stages add full characters. Some address contractions leave the TXID rule unchanged. The terminal script-hash rule is 75 leading bits toward repeated `7`: eighteen whole `7` digits followed by `6` or `7`, then any remaining valid hex digits. Address-space matching does not imply identical CPU cost for key derivation, script construction and valid-transaction generation.

### 3. Exact calendar and volume eligibility

`V(B)` counts all non-coinbase transactions in accepted blocks `H0+1` through `B`, inclusive; `V(H0)=0`. It counts neither retained records nor outputs. Coinbase transactions remain eligible for queries and admission but do not increment this counter. Integers MUST not overflow 32-bit counters or lose precision. Providers MUST disclose the provenance and verification/trust assumptions of their counts. Headers alone do not establish the cumulative transaction count; using a count service is a dependency, not a claim of header-only verification.

Set `G = T0 + 6 calendar months` and `E = T0 + 78 calendar months`, both added directly from `T0` in UTC, preserving time of day and clamping to the target month's last day. Define:

```text
w(i) = 11^(i-1) * 10^(26-i), for i = 1..26
W    = sum(w(i), i=1..26)
D    = E-G, in integer seconds
T(1) = G
T(s) = G + ceil(D * sum(w(i), i=1..s-1) / W), for s=2..27
candidate(B) = highest s with MTP(B) >= T(s) AND V(B) >= VOLUME[s],
               or 0 if none qualifies
```

The 26 post-runway intervals grow by approximately 10% after whole-second rounding. These are eligibility intervals, not empirical chain-growth estimates or guaranteed dwell times. Both gates must pass in the same accepted view. Insufficient volume can delay any stage indefinitely. A candidate is not an active stage: section 4 adds a fresh notice period to each actual contraction.

### 4. Deterministic notice rounds and following-block activation

Notice is a state of the accepted chain, not an action by an individual provider. A **notice round** freezes one target stage, its accepted triggering block, and a deadline exactly `1,209,600` MTP seconds (14 days) after that block's MTP. It starts automatically under the algorithm below; no publication, acknowledgment, provider participation or local timer is a precondition. All conforming providers using the same accepted tip MUST derive the same round and historical stages. This specification measures the notice period using accepted-chain median time, not a provider's wall clock or a block-count approximation. It does not guarantee 14 elapsed wall-clock days of warning for any observer.

Initialize stage 0 with no pending round at `H0`. Process accepted blocks parent before child:

```text
stage(B)   = stage(B.parent)
pending(B) = pending(B.parent)

if pending(B) exists AND MTP(B.parent) >= pending(B).deadline
                     AND candidate(B.parent) >= pending(B).target:
  stage(B) = pending(B).target
  pending(B) = none

process transactions in B using stage(B)
compute V(B), MTP(B), and candidate(B)

if pending(B) is none AND candidate(B) > stage(B):
  pending(B) = {
    target: candidate(B),
    triggerHash: hash(B), triggerHeight: height(B),
    startedAt: MTP(B), deadline: MTP(B) + 1209600
  }
```

Every block uses one event stage, including for funding and spends. A notice trigger cannot apply a new rule to its own transactions. Even the block first reaching the notice deadline uses the old rule; its accepted child activates the frozen target. The event stage is `stage(B)`, not `candidate(B.parent)`.

Higher eligibility during a pending round MUST NOT silently change its target or shorten its warning. After activation, a higher candidate may start a new round in that same block, with its own fresh 14-day deadline. Direct jumps remain permitted: when a round starts with candidate 17 and active stage 0, it announces a direct contraction to 17. It need not activate the skipped intermediate stages. If target 5 was already frozen when candidate 27 becomes eligible, the algorithm first activates 5, then opens a new round for 27. This prevents a newly eligible, more restrictive rule from borrowing an older target's notice.

The calendar table's 78-month value remains the final **eligibility** gate. With all volume thresholds met on time, the earliest final activation is no earlier than `E + 14 MTP days`, in the accepted child of a block reaching that notice deadline. Volume delays, a preceding pending round and block arrival can extend that. Actual contraction times may differ substantially from the nominal table intervals.

For a verified accepted tip `B`, the network is in a notice round exactly when `pending(B)` exists. Its remaining chain time is `max(0, pending(B).deadline - MTP(B))`. Zero remaining seconds means that the deadline has been reached but activation still awaits the following accepted block; it does not close the round or apply the target to the deadline-reaching block. If activation opens a higher-target round in that same block, that new round is the pending state. An unknown or unverified chain view cannot establish that no round exists. These are algorithmic facts, not required status fields or projected wall-clock activation dates.

A reorganization MUST recompute rounds solely from the replacement accepted history. An orphaned trigger contributes no elapsed time to a different replacement round. Conversely, a replacement branch may already contain its trigger, deadline-reaching block and activation child when first observed: its prescribed stage is then already active. Providers MUST NOT restart or extend the round from local discovery, demand that anyone published a notice while that branch was unknown, or carry an orphaned round into the replacement history. For example, a replacement branch with a target-5 trigger at MTP `2035-04-20T00:00:00Z`, deadline-reaching block at `2035-05-04T00:00:00Z` and accepted child uses stage 5 in that child, even if first learned on May 10. Recovery gaps require unavailable responses under section 5, not a different stage or a new notice clock.

Providers MUST document their adoption of the common coverage schedule and guarantees. They need not independently publish a stage notice or operate a new status interface, and no retroactive publication obligation arises from a reorganization, restart or late adoption. A provider entering the profile uses the common current stage and reconstructs admissions/spends using common historical stages; entry does not start a separate 14-day period. Separate customer commitments remain the provider's responsibility and do not alter this profile's algorithm. Operators SHOULD contact users early, communicate each pending target and its affected guarantees, publish migration/export instructions and use request-distribution logs to assess awareness. Communications crossing stage 13 SHOULD highlight the end of complete confirmed-spend accounting. These communications may use existing channels and need no API changes. Intentional latency changes or other business policies MUST be disclosed and cannot be used to claim an availability guarantee the operator is not delivering. Not every dormant user will see a communication; recovery consequences are described in section 8.

### 5. Accepted-chain authority and recovery retention

The currently accepted mainnet chain is the sole authority for `H0`, its MTP anchor, volume, notice rounds, stages, funding admission and spend-accounting effects. Rejected side branches have no effect. A reorganization MUST undo orphaned rounds and activations as well as transactions, then replay the replacement branch from a verified common ancestor. A replacement branch may lower the stage, remove a pending round, change which spend-accounting mode governed a transaction, or change funding admission. If `H0` changes, recompute all dependent state; if the chain no longer reaches it, the profile is inactive. The same accepted chain produces the same algorithmic stage and event-stage history for late joiners and existing providers.

Caches and durable checkpoints MUST bind hashes, ancestry, anchor, volume, active stage, pending round, query watermarks and ledger/watch state coherently. Heights, highest-ever stages and observed orphaned events cannot substitute for accepted ancestry. A restart MUST verify its checkpoint and replay/backfill any missing history before complete claims. Audit records about rejected branches may remain but MUST NOT affect current results.

Providers SHOULD retain enough durable recovery information for at least **100 accepted descendants** of the event that made it unnecessary for the active view. For retirement at accepted block `R` of height `H`, that recommendation protects it through `H+99`; `H+100` is the earliest removal consistent with that minimum, subject to longer needs. Use the effective activation block for contraction, the event block for a spend-state replacement, and the accepted entry snapshot for a late adopter withdrawing existing coverage. For retrieval-cap retirement, use the accepted snapshot in which the record first leaves the promised retrieval scope. Record hashes and verify ancestry: orphaned blocks, side-branch heights, wall-clock time and cached highest-seen heights do not age the recommendation. If `R` is orphaned, re-establish any retirement decision from the replacement history.

The recommendation covers affected history records, previous output/admission/spend state, per-block undo material and coherent checkpoints. An equivalent compressed or recovery-only representation is sufficient. Operators MAY keep data indefinitely or choose a different retention policy based on their risk assessment; they MUST publish the chosen minimum, any departure from the recommended 100 blocks and its recovery implications. The analogy is the [100-block coinbase maturity constant](https://github.com/bitcoin-sv/bitcoin-sv/blob/master/src/consensus/consensus.h), not a proof of finality or an estimate of reorganization probability. This is neither a compulsory purge at 100 blocks nor a promise that deeper reorganizations cannot occur.

Data still needed for live promised coverage MUST remain available, including grandfathered output state. Providers MUST NOT destroy the only usable data needed by an in-progress recovery while continuing to claim that recovery is supported. They are responsible for their own retained data or verified reconstruction arrangements. If a reorganization broadens coverage, reconstruct **all** newly required records within the declared retrieval/history scope and all admission/spend state needed by an offered ledger, including records predating the fork or `H0`. Restore the spend mode required by each historical block. Retrieval caps do not excuse missing ledger recovery state. A rollback from 13 to 12 can require backfilling spends previously outside the filtered feed. Until required state is verified, affected coverage is unavailable: use existing failure/availability mechanisms rather than assert a complete miss, zero balance or accurate UTXO state. No new recovery status code or API is required. No recovery duration or availability SLA is imposed here.

### 6. Free query coverage and commercial extensions

For a generalized funding/history record in the current free view:

```text
txidEligible(tx, s) = txidMatches(tx.txid, s)

addressRecordEligible(address, tx, vout, s) =
  tx.outputs[vout] pays exactly the canonical P2PKH script for address
  AND addressMatches(address, s) AND txidMatches(tx.txid, s)

scriptRecordEligible(scriptKey, tx, vout, s) =
  HASH160(tx.outputs[vout].lockingScript) == scriptKey
  AND scriptHashMatches(scriptKey, s) AND txidMatches(tx.txid, s)
```

Within the provider's declared retrieval/history scope, TXID lookup covers every qualifying accepted-chain transaction and address/script-hash history covers every qualifying funding/output record. Without a declared cap, that scope is genesis through the declared indexed-through block. Here history does not mean a complete list of all incoming and outgoing activity. A valid hit's transaction identifier and claimed accepted confirmation must be verified. A complete absence claim concerns only the declared scope and snapshot, not global nonexistence.

Providers MAY declare historical windows or record-count caps for TXID retrieval and address/script-hash history. They MUST document the affected operations, retained range or selection rule, record-count unit, ordering/tie boundaries where relevant, and how the cap interacts with stage filtering, advancing snapshots and reorganizations. For example, the newest 1,000 matching records and all matches within the newest 1,000 blocks are different coverage promises. Both may be offered, but an unstated limit cannot be treated as complete coverage. Records selected by the declared rule MUST remain freely obtainable through the provider's chosen delivery methods. Data excluded by that rule has no free retrieval/history guarantee, and missing ingestion inside the rule is a coverage failure rather than an additional cap. This neither requires pagination nor changes an existing API's format.

These caps apply to retrieval/history availability, **not** the admission or accounting of an offered output/balance ledger. Providers MUST still preserve and account for all admitted outputs while the query key remains eligible, including older grandfathered outputs whose transactions are no longer retrievable. They cannot silently drop an old coin, restrict the ledger to recent deposits, or claim a complete canonical amount for only the newest outputs. A provider unable to maintain that ledger may offer retrieval/history without the optional ledger service.

At activation, the guaranteed free keyspace contracts immediately. Outside it this BRC guarantees no free result. Providers may use existing error, access-control or broader-service arrangements; no new error code or response format is required. Their published coverage must make clear that failure to obtain an out-of-scope record does not establish its global absence. Broader paid or complimentary services have their own filters, pricing/entitlement and completeness/observation contract. This BRC's minimum guarantees do not automatically extend to that broader product. Physical retention or serving extra data does not make excluded data part of the guaranteed free scope.

Providers MAY retain a full index, share one backing database across products, or retain several broader stages. This profile contains no physical/logical deletion, data-layout or separate-database requirement. The economic objective is sustainable availability expectations, not compulsory destruction. Optional output and balance responses use section 7, whose grandfathering can retain funding references excluded from current generalized history/TXID lookup.

### 7. Output lists and balances: two spend-accounting modes

#### 7.1. Admission, grandfathering and key scope

An output is admitted to a key family's ledger when its funding transaction and that family's query key pass the event stage of the funding block. For earlier pre-activation history use stage 0. Evaluate historical admission at that block's event stage, not at the stage when a provider later discovers it. Admission for an address does not by itself admit the output to a script-hash ledger, or vice versa. Ignored nonqualifying deposits add no covered value and do not harm other admitted outputs.

Admitted output metadata MUST survive later funding-TXID contractions while needed for that ledger: keep the outpoint, amount, exact script/key relation, funding confirmation/admission stage and applicable spend state. The funding transaction may leave current generalized free lookup without removing this grandfathered state. An output-list response MAY identify its historical funding TXID even when generalized TXID lookup is outside the free keyspace. Current filtered history can consequently be empty while the ledger has positive value.

The query key itself is not grandfathered. If its address or script hash fails the current key rule, the free ledger promise ends; the absence of that guarantee is not a zero-balance claim. Active indexing/watch obligations for that key may stop, subject to the published recovery policy. A reorganization restoring the key requires verified historical reconstruction before its ledger guarantee resumes. There is no permanent disqualification for a nonqualifying spend, no poison-address rule, and no need to reset a key after a new qualifying deposit.

#### 7.2. Stages 0–12: complete confirmed-spend accounting

Providers offering output or balance operations MUST detect and account for every accepted confirmed spend of admitted outputs, irrespective of the spending TXID. They may use complete outpoint notifications rather than store every spending body, but a qualifying-TXID feed alone is insufficient for these stages. A nonqualifying spend simply debits the spent admitted output; it does not invalidate the key's remaining outputs or later deposits. Spending an ignored deposit does not debit the admitted ledger.

After complete admission and spend accounting through a common accepted snapshot, returned unspent admitted outputs may be described as **covered UTXOs**, and their integer-satoshi sum as a **covered balance**. This is still not the total balance of all deposits to the key: ignored deposits are excluded. Coinbase maturity and other spending restrictions remain distinct from unspent status and MAY be reported separately.

#### 7.3. Stages 13–27: qualifying-TXID spend accounting only

From stage 13 onward, an indexer MUST continue observing the complete qualifying-TXID stream and reflecting every accepted confirmed spend it contains of an admitted output, including grandfathered outputs. The spender's TXID is tested against the **event stage of its own accepted confirmation block**. A historically qualifying spend cannot be ignored retroactively because a later stage tightens the rule. A later spend of an old admitted output uses the later spend block's rule. All valid transactions within one block use that block's one event stage, with UTXO dependencies processed in valid transaction order.

For a transaction failing that event-stage TXID rule, the provider has no obligation to discover the spend. The baseline filtered ledger does not debit it, even if a provider learns of it through another source; additional validated debits belong to the separate enhanced view defined below. The baseline may therefore retain an output that has in fact already been spent and include its amount. It MUST remove every admitted output consumed by an accepted qualifying spend from subsequent snapshots. An unconfirmed transaction is not a confirmed debit; expose mempool observations separately. A transaction merely naming an input without validated accepted-chain spend evidence is insufficient.

The baseline guarantees **indexed TXOuts** and a **filtered-spend balance**, which may include genuinely spent outputs. Providers MUST document this reduced guarantee and MUST NOT advertise it as unconditional current unspentness or proof of spendability. Existing endpoint and field names such as `utxos`, `unspent` or `balance` may remain unchanged; these explanatory terms do not mandate renaming, new flags, per-response labels or a new API. The guarantee changes, while the provider's chosen delivery format can stay the same.

Staleness can accumulate through repeated circulation, not merely leave one old deposit visible. Suppose a key has an admitted 100-satoshi output. After stage 13, a nonqualifying transaction spends it back to that key as 99 satoshis, with 1 satoshi consumed as a fee. The old 100 may remain in the ledger while the new 99 is ignored. A qualifying transaction then spends that ignored output back to the key as 98 satoshis after another 1-satoshi fee. Its new output is admitted, but its input is not the original admitted outpoint: the filtered amount may now be 198 while the actual unspent amount is 98. Repeating with a nonqualifying spend to 97 and a qualifying spend to 96 can produce a filtered amount of 294 while only 96 remains unspent. These illustrative amounts do not prescribe a viable fee rate or transaction construction. The effect creates no coins and changes no consensus rule; it shows why a filtered ledger is not a measure of actual holdings, solvency or spending capacity.

To keep this ledger equal to the real unspent subset of its admitted funds, an owner MUST ensure that **every actual spending transaction consuming an admitted output**, including pre-signed, delegated or delayed transactions, qualifies under its accepted spend block's stage. Transacting parties requiring funding admission or qualifying-spend accounting MUST ensure that the **final confirmed transaction's TXID** passes the applicable filter. The owner's responsibility includes choosing transaction versions, scripts, signature-hash modes and signing/serialization workflows compatible with that requirement. A matching unsigned template, signed candidate or broadcast TXID is insufficient if the accepted serialized transaction has a different, nonqualifying TXID. Indexers test the actual accepted TXID; equivalent payment intent or authorization does not substitute for the predicate or impose additional spend-monitoring duties on them.

Transaction malleability is therefore an application constraint. The [SV Node 1.2.0 Chronicle release notes](https://github.com/bitcoin-sv/bitcoin-sv/releases/tag/v1.2.0) describe relaxed malleability restrictions for transaction versions greater than 1 and preservation of those restrictions for version 1. An owner relying on TXID filtering may consequently need to restrict its workflow to `version = 1`; this BRC does not assert that version 1 alone prevents all malleability for every script, signature mode or accepted transaction construction. Parties MUST evaluate the final-transaction requirement for their complete spending workflow. If they cannot ensure qualification, they cannot rely on the corresponding admission or post-stage-12 spend guarantee. This is a condition of free indexing coverage, not a new consensus validity rule.

Owners cannot infer that every spend qualified merely from a positive filtered balance. Clients SHOULD establish actual spend status independently of the filtered response, using their own validated records or appropriate spend-status checks, before selecting outputs or treating its amount as actual holdings or evidence of solvency. This is advisory client guidance, not an additional free-indexer monitoring obligation or a mandatory condition for querying the filtered service. Parties MAY target a narrower forthcoming keyspace and pay a fee more likely to obtain timely inclusion; neither a higher fee nor the following-block activation rule guarantees a confirmation time.

The **baseline** ledger is canonical: apply the historical admissions, every confirmed spend during stages 0–12, and only qualifying-TXID confirmed spends during stages 13–27. Complete baseline results for the same family, key and accepted snapshot MUST have the same output membership and amount, independent of optional information a provider possesses. A provider MUST offer this baseline for each output/balance operation it claims under the profile and MUST NOT silently substitute an enhanced result. This determinism concerns the defined filtered ledger, not actual unspentness.

A provider MAY separately offer an **enhanced** service that starts from the baseline's admissions and applies additional validated nonqualifying spends, for example from a paid backing index or targeted status checks. Its published guarantee MUST distinguish it from the canonical baseline and describe the extra observations and their completeness. Existing service tiers, agreements or delivery mechanisms may make that distinction; no view selector, additional endpoint or response field is required. It MUST NOT resurrect baseline debits or include ignored deposits while claiming the same admission coverage; broader admissions need their own contract under section 6. Providers' enhanced results may differ because their extra observations differ. A known spent output retained in a baseline result remains an indexed TXOut, never a guarantee of actual unspentness.

After stage 12, an enhanced service with only additional partial spend observations still has no complete-unspentness guarantee. Finding extra spends does not establish the absence of others. A service claiming complete actual-unspentness coverage must separately document and satisfy that stronger observation contract; this profile does not infer it from the enhanced description.

Baseline comparisons require the same profile, family, key and accepted snapshot; enhanced comparisons additionally require matching extra observation contracts and evidence. If a provider promises a consistent multi-part result, newly learned extra spends must not silently change that result between parts. It may preserve the original result or use its existing failure/retry mechanism. This is a consistency requirement on an offered guarantee, not a requirement for pagination, exposed revision identifiers or any particular delivery mechanism.

#### 7.4. State transitions, truthful snapshots and reorganizations

Entering stage 13 changes the observation promise for all still-admitted outputs, including grandfathered ones. Providers may stop the whole-network spend watch after that activation while maintaining the qualifying stream. Debits correctly established during stages 0–12 MUST remain debited; the transition does not resurrect previously spent outputs. New qualifying funding can still be admitted, and ignored incoming payments still remain excluded.

| Accepted event | Required effect |
| --- | --- |
| Nonqualifying funding deposit | Do not admit it; other admitted funds are unaffected. |
| Later TXID rule excludes an admitted funding TXID | Preserve grandfathered ledger state while the key remains eligible. |
| Any confirmed spend of an admitted output during stages 0–12 | Debit that output, regardless of spender TXID. |
| Confirmed qualifying spend during stages 13–27 | Debit every consumed admitted output; do not return it as a UTXO or count its value. |
| Confirmed nonqualifying spend during stages 13–27 | No baseline debit; an enhanced view may apply a separately disclosed validated debit. |
| Spend of only ignored funds | No debit to the admitted ledger. |
| Mixed admitted/ignored inputs | Apply the spend rule separately to each admitted input; do not retire the key. |
| Reorganization removes a funding or accounted spend | Undo it and replay admission/debits using replacement-block stages. |
| Reorganization restores a stage below 13 | Re-establish complete confirmed-spend accounting; unavailable until backfill completes. |

A ledger snapshot MUST lie on the accepted chain and be complete for the admission and spend-observation scope it claims. A lost qualifying-stream interval after stage 13 is a real coverage gap, not permission to ignore required spends; backfill it before complete filtered-ledger claims. Likewise, a stages-0–12 watch gap forbids a verified covered balance. Providers MAY offer older verified as-of snapshots but MUST NOT claim current coverage unless their required observations reach the current accepted tip. Missing data is unavailable, never a fabricated zero. Published availability guarantees MUST distinguish stage-13 intentional exclusion from an ingestion failure, without requiring new response labels.

For an as-of result, distinguish the current accepted tip `B` from the ledger snapshot block `Q`, which MUST be `B` or its accepted ancestor. The stage at `B` governs the query key's present free eligibility; an older snapshot cannot restore a key excluded now. The stage at `Q` governs the baseline observation guarantee for that historical ledger. Admission and required spend accounting MUST both be complete through `Q`, with no later ledger effects included in its amount. A verified snapshot from stages 0–12 may still provide a **covered balance as of block Q**, even when the current stage is 13 or later. That guarantee establishes unspentness only through `Q`, never through the later tip. Snapshots from stage 13 onward retain section 7.3's reduced spend-accounting guarantee.

For example, a still-eligible key at current stage 13 can have a verified covered amount at an earlier stage-12 block. A later spend does not change that historical amount, but the service MUST NOT represent it as a current-unspentness guarantee. Where a provider offers historical results, its existing delivery context or documentation MUST unambiguously associate them with their as-of snapshot and distinguish them from current data. Providers need not add a historical-query operation or metadata fields to an existing API; if their delivery cannot make that distinction, they cannot use an old result as a substitute for promised current availability. A reorganization removing `Q` invalidates that snapshot's accepted-chain guarantee.

### 8. Migration, recovery and services still required

#### 8.1. End-to-end transaction and recovery path

The [Bitcoin white paper](https://bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf), particularly sections 7 and 8, describes storage reclamation and verification with headers and Merkle branches, including dependence on honest network behavior and the need to handle invalidity alerts. The author treats SPV and direct exchange as the intended scalable end-user configuration. This BRC does not claim that inclusion proves current unspentness, that archival data becomes unnecessary to everyone, or that every business must run the same wallet implementation.

An implementer can build the following path from open specifications and source. These are distinct responsibilities, each of which needs operating resources:

1. **Construct and deliver the relevant transaction.** Use [BRC-29](../payments/0029.md) and [BRC-42](../key-derivation/0042.md) where applicable. A BRC-100 sender constructs with `createAction`, preserves the transaction and remittance/derivation metadata, and delivers them to the recipient or its chosen durable inbox. A receiving wallet uses `internalizeAction` to validate and retain its relevant outputs. An offline recipient needs a retained delivery route and retry/reconciliation policy, not a hope that a future address scan will find everything.
2. **Carry and validate evidence.** [BRC-62](../transactions/0062.md) BEEF, [BRC-95](../transactions/0095.md) Atomic BEEF and [BRC-67](../transactions/0067.md) describe transaction/ancestry/SPV handling. Preserve the bytes and required parents/proofs; verify identifiers, scripts, amounts and Merkle paths against the accepted header chain. Missing evidence must be obtained from the counterparty, retained store or an explicitly supported retrieval service. BEEF is not a promise that an arbitrary missing body can be recovered for free.
3. **Broadcast and track the submitted transaction.** Arcade's [implemented Teranode integration](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/arcade/blob/e48baded86b3ea8c934f0509ba2cd3927b765376/doc.md) submits transactions, follows network lifecycle events, registers tracked transactions for proof delivery and exposes status/callback interfaces. Submission acknowledgement is not confirmation or finality. Applications must reconcile later acceptance, rejection, conflicts, proofs and reorganization state.
4. **Acquire and preserve proofs.** Arcade's [current API contract](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/arcade/blob/e48baded86b3ea8c934f0509ba2cd3927b765376/openapi/arcade.openapi.yaml) carries mined Merkle paths and chain/reorganization interfaces. Proof delivery is tied to tracked transactions and configured upstream Merkle Service availability. The Toolbox [Arcade provider implementation](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/ts-stack/blob/9262cac6609ffa86145f7679a00b8d012e1cb6c7/packages/wallet/wallet-toolbox/src/services/providers/Arcade.ts) validates returned paths against its chaintracker and falls through when tracked proof data is absent. A lifecycle store keyed by transactions it was asked to handle is distinct from a promise of universal historical TXID retrieval. Preserve received evidence so future arbitrary historical proof requests are unnecessary for ordinary recovery.
5. **Track headers and accepted-chain changes.** [ChainTracks](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/ts-stack/blob/9262cac6609ffa86145f7679a00b8d012e1cb6c7/docs/infrastructure/chaintracks-server.md) supplies bulk/live headers, validation and tip/reorganization interfaces. Header integrity, work, linkage, source trust and synchronization still require implementation. On a reorganization, invalidate orphaned confirmations/proofs, refresh evidence and reconcile transaction/output state. A stale header source cannot establish a current view merely because an old inclusion proof still hashes correctly.
6. **Check and reconcile spend/conflict state.** Known-outpoint UTXO/spend checks, broadcast conflict handling, double-spend detection/deconfliction and relevant notifications remain necessary. They are scoped to transactions and outputs the parties care about, not necessarily an all-address discovery database. Inclusion and token-lineage proofs do not establish current UTXO membership. Treat a missing response, lag or inconclusive rejection as unknown; do not free reservations or promise spendability without appropriate evidence.
7. **Replicate and export wallet data independently of keys.** [BRC-38](../outpoints/0038.md) defines portable per-user records, [BRC-39](../outpoints/0039.md) protects the file, and [BRC-40](../outpoints/0040.md) defines incremental storage synchronization. Toolbox [storage code](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/ts-stack/blob/9262cac6609ffa86145f7679a00b8d012e1cb6c7/packages/wallet/wallet-toolbox/src/storage/WalletStorageManager.ts) provides `syncFromReader` and `updateBackups`. Use complete, verified synchronization and consistent versioned backups. Replication can replicate deletion or corruption; it is not by itself an independent retained backup.
8. **Restore with both authority and records.** The [wallet recovery guide](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/ts-stack/blob/9262cac6609ffa86145f7679a00b8d012e1cb6c7/docs/guides/wallet-backup-recovery.md) separates root-key recovery, transaction/derivation records, product configuration and backup access. The [portability implementation guide](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/ts-stack/blob/9262cac6609ffa86145f7679a00b8d012e1cb6c7/docs/guides/wallet-data-portability.md) identifies real export/import helpers and limits, including consistency and provider compatibility. Restore into an independently controlled compatible store, reconcile proofs and spend state, and rehearse loss of the original device and provider. Neither a seed nor a BRC-39 file alone supplies both halves.

Purpose-built overlays receive transactions for their topics rather than discovering every payment on chain. The open [Overlay Engine](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/ts-stack/blob/9262cac6609ffa86145f7679a00b8d012e1cb6c7/docs/packages/overlays/overlay.md) accepts submitted BEEF, applies topic admission and lookup rules and supports [BRC-76](../transactions/0076.md) graph-aware synchronization; [BRC-88](../overlays/0088.md) describes synchronization architecture. Operators still preserve relevant history, propagate spends/transitions and run their chosen storage and recovery processes. An overlay's topical index is not the universal free script-hash index this profile contracts.

Token protocols can require additional evidence: [BRC-176](../tokens/0176.md) preserves token-specific parent bodies back to the applicable deploy/authority for BSV-21/1Color lineage. Ordinary Atomic BEEF's SPV-minimal omission rules are insufficient for that packet. Parties must retain the evidence required by the token protocol as well as ordinary Bitcoin validation data; a token-validity packet does not prove unspent status or global supply.

#### 8.2. Implemented, deployed and planned capabilities

As of the research date, the pinned Toolbox, SDK, Overlay Engine, ChainTracks and Arcade sources implement these building blocks. A public [July 2026 Storage–Arcade–Monitor integration report](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/ts-stack/pull/244) records a bounded mainnet run in which 29 transactions mined, received proofs and reached completed wallet-storage state, and also records later failed load attempts and corrective work. It establishes an exercised deployed path, not a throughput ceiling, universal wallet compatibility, successful recovery under every failure, or proof that all current dependencies have already migrated.

Current Toolbox [service defaults](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/ts-stack/blob/9262cac6609ffa86145f7679a00b8d012e1cb6c7/packages/wallet/wallet-toolbox/src/services/Services.ts) still include explorer-backed raw transaction, script-hash history, proof and UTXO-status services. Configurability and open source do not erase those dependencies automatically. Deployments must inventory which operations rely on them, retain/deliver required records, and replace, self-operate or commercially fund necessary retrieval/status services before removing a fallback they still need. No particular vendor or endpoint is mandated by this BRC, and no present public endpoint is promised to remain free.

[BRC-83](../transactions/0083.md) describes hierarchical transaction/graph aggregation and downstream proof completion. [BRC-80](./0080.md) describes version/TXID multicast subscription concepts. These are directions for further capacity and efficiency investment, not assertions that this exact profile's transport is already deployed. BRC-80's illustrative mapping carries 48 TXID bits; the final filter here needs 52, requiring broader-shard receipt and local filtering or a transport extension. All-version subscriptions, completeness, loss recovery and proof-distribution economics also need an actual binding. Responsible planning can use implemented direct-delivery systems now while investing in those improvements; it need not promise that every future component works perfectly today.

#### 8.3. Necessary roles and optional universal services

| Still needed for the selected use case | Responsibility/cost |
| --- | --- |
| Private keys, derivation/spending metadata and compatible signing logic | User/wallet custody and tested recovery; an index is not a key backup. |
| Relevant transaction and token ancestry, proof/broadcast state and durable delivery | Parties and chosen storage/inbox/overlay operators preserve and exchange it. |
| Header services, accepted-chain validation and reorganization alerting | Independently verified chain view with explicit source and availability assumptions. |
| Transaction broadcast, proof acquisition, known-outpoint spend checks and double-spend deconfliction | Transaction processors and scoped service providers under sustainable operating arrangements. |
| Storage synchronization, consistent backups and provider exit | Wallet/storage operators and users verify both data recovery and key recovery. |

The author does not consider universal free full-history TXID lookup, all-address/script-hash balance or UTXO enumeration, bulk historical block delivery, or arbitrary on-demand old Merkle proofs necessary prerequisites for every participant. Such products may remain valuable and may charge for retention, computation and delivery. This version contracts only the free index families it defines; it does not remove node validation, abolish archives, or set retirement rules for those other services. Future specifications may address their own free-availability expectations separately.

An owner returning with only a seed has no guaranteed free recovery outside the current keyspace, and post-stage-12 filtered results cannot establish actual unspentness even inside it. Existing wallet records, retained backups, counterparties or a willing historical/paid provider may enable reconstruction, without changing consensus ownership. This BRC cannot promise that a suitable provider exists, retains everything or will quote an affordable price. Operators should facilitate export/migration before withdrawal. The author accepts that missed migration can make recovery more costly or unavailable through the user's former service; the contract must state that consequence rather than imply that coins were invalidated or that keys alone solve it.

### 9. Availability conformance and existing services

Conformance is assessed against the data a provider promises to make freely available, for each offered family and operation. A conforming TXID service makes every currently qualifying accepted-chain transaction within its declared retrieval limits available. A conforming address or script-hash history service makes the corresponding qualifying records within its declared history limits available. An offered output/balance service maintains the complete canonical admitted ledger and required spend accounting independently of those retrieval/history limits, including grandfathering, the stage-13 boundary and accepted-chain rollback. Required data cannot be silently omitted while claiming complete coverage. Exact coverage calculations and amounts MUST retain integer precision internally.

The provider's existing APIs and delivery methods may continue unchanged, including existing transaction, history, output and balance formats. Neither WhatsOnChain's format nor any other provider's format defines conformance. This BRC requires no particular HTTP behavior, field names, status vocabulary, headers, error representation, view selector, revision identifier, pagination scheme or machine-readable status interface. Providers need not introduce a new operation or change what their existing fields are named. The reference vectors' JSON keys and classifications describe decoded test state only; they are not an API contract.

An adopting provider MUST document which guarantees it offers under BRC-234 version 1, including the coverage schedule, any optional output/balance service, reduced spend accounting after stage 12, any separately enhanced service, and its recovery/completeness policies. Existing documentation and communication channels are sufficient. No per-response metadata or separately published contraction notice is required. Historical or enhanced data must not be substituted for a different advertised availability guarantee; sections 7.3–7.4 define that distinction without prescribing how the provider delivers it.

During an ingestion or recovery gap, the affected required data is unavailable until verified reconstruction. Providers may use their existing failure/retry mechanisms, but MUST NOT advertise complete coverage or fabricate a complete empty result, zero amount or current-unspentness guarantee for that gap. Data outside the current free scope has no availability guarantee under this profile, regardless of whether a provider can physically retain or commercially serve it. The BRC specifies no new response for either situation.

A provider MUST NOT advertise live conformance while permanently withholding the required in-scope data. A policy guaranteeing no data for an advertised operation is withdrawal of that operation, not a resource cap preserving its availability. This BRC sets no universal SLA and does not compel eternal service. Internal checkpoints and delivery consistency must support the guarantees actually advertised; conformance does not require exposing those internals through a new API.

Provider-specific closure, conversion to paid-only access, withdrawal of an offered family/operation, or a change in declared retrieval/history caps is governed by that provider's existing service terms. The provider MUST communicate the change through its existing channels and cease claiming guarantees it no longer delivers. This BRC imposes no additional minimum notice duration, local 14-day timer or required communication format for those service decisions. They do not change the network's stage, event history or notice rounds. While a provider continues claiming this profile for a service, its then-advertised scope must satisfy the applicable availability guarantees.

#### 9.1. Feed completeness and dependency contract

For every offered family and operation, providers MUST publish how they establish completeness of its required observations. The contract MUST identify the data-source roles, provenance and trust assumptions; accepted-chain/confirmation validation; transaction-version and filter coverage; continuity and gap detection; historical backfill and reorganization reconciliation; and the basis of each completeness watermark. It MUST distinguish locally verified complete blocks from an upstream filtered source's assertion of completeness, or document any other method used. Source dependencies may be replaceable; publishing this contract neither mandates a vendor nor promises that a current endpoint will remain available.

A completeness watermark is an accepted block hash/height through which **all required observations in the declared scope** have been accounted for without a known gap. It is not the highest block seen, the confirmation height of the most recently delivered match, a connection heartbeat or a count of matching records. A block with no qualifying transactions still needs a completeness basis: silence alone does not establish an empty result. A header or a returned transaction's Merkle proof authenticates neither delivery of all matches nor the absence of omitted transactions. Providers relying on a trusted filtered source MUST state that residual completeness trust rather than imply that inclusion proofs eliminate it.

Coverage MUST include every accepted block and transaction version relevant to the claimed historical query or ledger interval, including qualifying coinbase transactions. Retrieval/history completeness is relative to the declared caps; an offered ledger's admission and spend completeness is not. Historical replay MUST use the required event-stage filters and spend modes; a present narrower subscription cannot establish the completeness of an earlier broader interval. Stages 0–12 require both complete admission observations and every spend of admitted outputs. Stages 13–27 require complete admission observations and every qualifying-TXID spend of admitted outputs, including grandfathered ones. Extra enhanced observations have a separately disclosed scope/watermark; gaps in optional extra observations cannot silently change or invalidate the canonical baseline.

Providers MUST document how they detect discontinuity or source failure, resume or backfill missing intervals, and reconcile source checkpoints with accepted block hashes after a reorganization. No cryptographic completeness or omission-detection mechanism is invented here: the contract must candidly identify any dependence on an upstream source's correct gap reporting. A known gap, unresolved fork identity or missing required historical interval forbids a complete claim beyond the last supported watermark. Return unavailable for an affected requested snapshot, or an explicitly historical verified snapshot under section 7.4; never a complete miss, zero amount or unqualified current result. A gap wholly after a historical snapshot need not invalidate that earlier snapshot.

This requirement does not restore whole-network spend monitoring after stage 12. An indexer may obtain the complete qualifying stream from processors, filtered feeds or another documented arrangement, while upstream services perform any broader processing their arrangement needs. It must establish the promised scope under its disclosed assumptions, not independently retain or scan every nonqualifying transaction. The provider MUST document its completeness/watermark policy and known limitations of the availability it advertises. This requires no watermark API, new status interface, contract identifier field or change to an existing response format.

## Appendix A: Complete schedule and expected search work

Both time and volume gates must pass, then the fresh 14-MTP-day notice round must complete. `Gate` and `Interval` below are approximate nominal months relative to `T0`, calculated as `6 + 72` times the exact post-runway cumulative weight fraction; eligibility dates themselves use section 3's calendar endpoints and integer-second formula. They are **not activation times**. Stage 0 has unrestricted keys and zero post-anchor volume. The first six months are a separate runway.

Patterns constrain only the prefix of a valid full key. Address ranges use ordered Base58 `123456789ABCDEFGHJKLMNPQRSTUVWXYZabcdefghijkmnopqrstuvwxyz`; hex ranges use hexadecimal order. The explicit address sets and bit counts in section 2 are authoritative.

Expected key trials assume uniform public-key HASH160 and counted valid Base58Check encodings. Expected script trials assume uniform script HASH160 with a usable script; expected TX trials assume independently generated valid transaction candidates. These are means, not work limits, runtime claims or evidence that every arbitrary script can be varied safely.

The final address mean is about `3.3249e22` candidate keys, script mean `2^75 = 37,778,931,862,957,161,709,568` candidate scripts, and TXID mean `2^52 = 4,503,599,627,370,496` valid transaction candidates. They are distinct operations. A transaction candidate may require re-signing. Qualifying script generation must preserve the intended spending conditions; a qualifying digest alone is not a spendable output.

An address or script can be ground once and reused while transaction candidates are varied. Do not multiply these columns as the cost of that staged strategy. A user needing both address and script-hash coverage for the same P2PKH output must satisfy both independent key predicates; the separate means do not estimate that combined search. Grinding/reuse/targeted traffic can invalidate uniform-traffic storage projections. Previously admitted outputs do not need their historical funding TXIDs changed; later spend observability follows the spend block's stage and the two-mode contract.
