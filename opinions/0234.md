# BRC-234: Progressive Contraction of Free Chain-Scanned Index Coverage

Ty Everett (ty@projectbabbage.com)

> **Status:** Proposed specification, version 1. This is a voluntary contract for free TXID, address and script-hash query coverage. It does not assert universal adoption, provider availability, measured savings, or deployment of every future transport discussed below. Research status: 2026-10-20.

## Abstract

This BRC proposes coordinated contraction of the keyspace for which participating BSV mainnet indexers promise free access. It covers generalized TXID retrieval and address/script-hash history, output-list and balance queries. Block `H0 = 1,000,000` anchors 27 contractions, each requiring both calendar and cumulative transaction-volume gates. Every actual contraction additionally has a deterministic, stage-specific 14-day notice round. The final calendar gate is 78 months after the anchor; notice, block arrival and insufficient volume can delay activation.

Address or script-hash coverage requires the corresponding key filter **and** the funding transaction's TXID filter. TXID lookup requires only the TXID filter, regardless of contained scripts or addresses. Script-hash coverage uses `HASH160(lockingScript)` and a growing leading-bit constraint toward repeated hexadecimal `7`, with expected uniform-hash grinding work between the address work and twice that work. The terminal address and TXID prefixes are `1ChickenNugget` and thirteen `9`s.

Stages 0–12 require complete confirmed-spend accounting for admitted outputs. From stage 13, only spends whose TXIDs meet their event-stage rule must be reflected. Returned outputs and amounts can consequently be stale relative to the real UTXO set, and MUST be explicitly labeled as filtered-spend results. There is no whole-address or whole-script-hash disqualification mechanism. Previously admitted outputs remain grandfathered across later funding-TXID contractions while their query key remains eligible.

Accepted-chain history alone governs stage and ledger state; reorganizations undo orphaned notice rounds, activations, admissions and spend effects. Providers SHOULD retain recovery data for at least 100 accepted descendants before destructive removal, disclose their actual policy, and return unavailable during any unreconstructed gap. No provision requires purging data or prohibits a shared complete backing index, a paid archive, or paid access to broader keyspaces.

## Motivation and rationale

### The reliability contract being retired

The primary concern is the expectation that comprehensive indexing will remain available free of charge, indefinitely, as part of an application's reliability and recovery contract. Historical transaction lookup entries accumulate as transactions accumulate. Address and script indexes add output records, spend state, query work, replication, bandwidth and operational recovery costs. Transaction growth can accelerate these burdens; this proposal does not claim that every index's cost follows a proven exponential curve. A free API does not make its operation costless: an operator, sponsor, other customer or another participant still bears the bill.

The author considers that arrangement increasingly misaligned with a growing BSV blockchain. An application should not externalize indefinite retention, discovery and recovery obligations onto parties that receive no corresponding revenue. A private key supplies signing authority; it does not supply the outpoints, transaction bodies, derivation instructions, current spend state or validation evidence needed to use that authority. A seed alone is not an entitlement to free rediscovery of all those records at arbitrary future scale.

A secondary concern is completeness trust. An index can omit payments or spends, lag, or present incomplete balances. An inclusion proof can authenticate a returned transaction but does not prove that an address's history is complete or that an output remains unspent. Multiple independent providers can improve resilience, but agreement is not a cryptographic proof against correlated omission. Direct delivery and locally validated evidence reduce the need to discover one's transactions through that intermediary; they do not abolish chain, availability or conflict-detection dependencies.

### Why a shared contraction profile

Independent, incompatible filters could leave applications unable to use the same keys across providers. A common sequence of nested keyspaces preserves a shared pool while free guarantees shrink. The accepted chain determines the same notice rounds and stages for every conforming provider at the same tip. Operators still have to provide complete data for their declared scope and snapshot; a common filter alone does not establish correctness, capacity or availability.

The policy deliberately favors migration to direct delivery, peer-to-peer transaction exchange and purpose-built overlays. The author's architectural position is that use cases can be designed around those methods without requiring a universal free index. This is a design position, not a claim that every application has completed its migration or that every proposed infrastructure component is deployed. Paid indexing is an expressly legitimate alternative, including for explorers, exchanges, researchers, auditors and historical recovery.

A provider may offer current stage `n` free, stage `n-1` for a fee, a broader stage `n-m` for another fee, or the whole keyspace commercially. Revenue may subsidize the free portion. This BRC sets no prices and regulates no internal storage layout: one complete backing index may serve all of these products. Success includes the continued existence of sustainable comprehensive paid services. The desired outcome is the end of reliance on an unpriced, universal, perpetual fallback, with explicit responsibility for preserving and paying for necessary data.

### Evidence and its limits

The operational burden is concrete. BSV's [SV Node storage guidance](https://docs.bsvblockchain.org/network-topology/nodes/sv-node/installation/sv-node/aws-volumes-setup) described approximately 12 TB of block data and 350 GB of chainstate in January 2024, with performance-sensitive UTXO storage distinct from cheaper block storage. These are dated node-storage figures, not a current measurement of an explorer index or a forecast of this proposal's savings. The association's [May 2024 report](https://resources.bsvblockchain.org/hubfs/BSV%20report/BSV%20blockchain%20digital%20transformation%20-%20The%20Role%20of%20Blockchain%20in%20Safeguarding%20Data%20and%20Streamlining%20Transactions.pdf) reported over 3.6 billion processed transactions. Historical records grow cumulatively; UTXO-set size can rise or fall with output creation and spending and MUST NOT be presented as monotonically growing merely because history grows.

Blockchair's [June 2022 operator account](https://github.com/Blockchair/Blockchair.Support/issues/910#issuecomment-1159369293) described large-block transfer, duplicated explorer databases, disk/development costs and too little BSV API demand to cover them. Its [later support response](https://github.com/Blockchair/Blockchair.Support/issues/1045#issuecomment-1504229868) confirmed limiting full public BSV support on 2022-11-07. The same incident included node-version and database problems; it is evidence of that operator's experience, not proof of a universal technical ceiling. The author's economic inference is that unpaid comprehensive coverage is an unreliable long-term application dependency.

Exchange support can also end, but causation must be attributed honestly. For example, [Kraken's 2019 announcement](https://blog.kraken.com/product/kraken-is-delisting-bsv) gave conduct-related reasons. This BRC does not claim that exchange delistings generally establish index-cost causation. For exchanges choosing to support BSV, receiving relevant transactions and proofs directly can reduce the need for full-history discovery; exchange-specific security, accounting, liquidity and operating requirements remain their responsibility.

No measured global cost saving or statistically calibrated optimum is asserted. The author proposes the anchor, six-month initial runway, 78-month final calendar gate, 10% interval growth and volume milestones as reasonable starting policy judgments. Future proposals may argue for alternatives. Raw transaction count directly relates to the number of TXID lookup entries in a complete transaction index, although byte cost, output count, query load and hardware also matter. Volume is a scale gate, not proof of migration readiness; generated traffic counts too and cannot advance the calendar gates.

### Intended end state and tradeoffs

Early stages provide time to prepare before extreme grinding becomes necessary. Later grinding is intentionally prohibitive for ordinary legacy use. Its cost is borne by the party insisting on continued free-index eligibility; the author's expectation is that direct delivery or paid access will become preferable. This is not a promise of a particular future service price, key-search throughput or transaction-generation cost. Grinding is a transition pressure, not a resource-saving computation for its own sake, and no hard upper bound on admitted traffic is claimed.

The fixed final filters remain part of version 1. The intended economic outcome is that essentially no ordinary use remains, allowing operators eventually to close the free legacy product or offer remaining users paid service. Actual closure is a separate service decision with notice; this version has no mandatory shutdown stage. Providers do not promise to operate forever merely by adopting it.

The terminal strings are arbitrary and carry no cryptographic privilege. Repeated `9`s and `7`s are recognizable; Chicken Nugget is the name of the author's sister's kitten. The author states that no qualifying infrastructure was prepared in advance. The strings are public predicates anyone may target, not registrations or allocations to chosen participants.

## Specification

**MUST**, **MUST NOT**, **SHOULD** and **MAY** apply to services voluntarily claiming conformance. A provider MUST identify which free index families and optional output/balance operations it offers. Conformance concerns their advertised availability and observation guarantees, not ownership, consensus validity, custody, internal database representation or commercial pricing.

`Free` means the advertised profile scope is obtainable without paying for that query or purchasing a broader indexing entitlement. Providers MAY require authentication and publish reasonable access, rate and resource limits; they MUST disclose them and MUST NOT use them to conceal that the claimed free service is effectively absent. Paid priority, bulk delivery and broader coverage remain separate offers. This profile is not a promise of unlimited free bandwidth, arbitrary historical proof work or an availability SLA.

### 1. Canonical keys and independent query families

The profile applies to BSV mainnet. `MTP(B)` is the median Unix timestamp of accepted block `B` and its ten immediate ancestors. `T0 = MTP(H0)` is the UTC anchor. No publication date or forecast supplies that value. Before the accepted chain reaches `H0`, there is no activated profile and historical admission uses unrestricted stage 0.

A P2PKH address MUST be a canonical, checksum-valid mainnet Base58Check encoding of version byte `0x00` and a 20-byte public-key HASH160. Its output script is exactly `76a914{20-byte public-key hash}88ac`. The checksum is the first four bytes of double-SHA256 of version plus payload. Validate decoded length, version, checksum and canonical re-encoding. Do not fold case or trim whitespace. Other script templates are outside the address family but may use the script-hash family.

The script coverage key is `RIPEMD160(SHA256(S))`, where `S` is the exact serialized locking-script byte string. This is the hash of the script, not a public-key hash extracted from it. Its canonical display is 40 hexadecimal characters in digest-byte order, most significant bit first; uppercase input is accepted and normalized to lowercase. No byte reversal, `0x`, padding, truncation or whitespace is permitted. All locking-script templates may use this family; hashing an arbitrary string is not evidence that its script can be spent.

Existing APIs using SHA256, reversed hashes or other identifiers MUST derive this 20-byte coverage key from the actual script, verify the relation to their native query identifier, and disclose the mapping. A 32-byte identifier cannot simply be truncated or relabeled. If the script/key relation cannot be established, return unavailable or unsupported rather than inventing eligibility. An API MAY require the exact script as a query parameter. Script-hash records MUST identify the exact script of each output; hash equality alone is not a claim that the caller controls every matching script.

TXIDs MUST be exactly 64 hexadecimal characters without whitespace or `0x`, normalized to lowercase. Apply leading-bit tests to conventional big-endian display order, not to a little-endian internal buffer.

```text
leadingBitsMatch(validatedHex, repeatedDigit, requiredBits):
  t = lowercase(validatedHex)
  whole = requiredBits // 4
  partial = requiredBits % 4
  if t[0:whole] != repeatedDigit repeated whole times: return false
  if partial == 0: return true
  return (hexValue(t[whole]) >> (4-partial)) ==
         (hexValue(repeatedDigit) >> (4-partial))

txidMatches(txid, stage) = leadingBitsMatch(txid, "9", TXID_BITS[stage])
scriptHashMatches(h, stage) = leadingBitsMatch(h, "7", SCRIPT_BITS[stage])
```

Address and script-hash eligibility are independent alternatives: neither family additionally requires the other's key predicate. Each additionally requires the funding TXID predicate for generalized history/admission. TXID eligibility is independent of both key families. Raw transaction retrieval returns the complete transaction, never redacted addresses or scripts. A provider supporting only one family MUST NOT imply that it offers the others.

The script-hash family includes generalized public lookups exposed through raw-script queries or alternative script identifiers; changing the API key representation does not exempt equivalent script-based discovery. Private wallet records, topical overlays and separately offered broader services retain their own contracts. This BRC does not contract the wallet's own authenticated list of transactions it received directly.

Let `N(s)` be the exact number of 20-byte public-key HASH160 values whose mainnet Base58Check addresses satisfy stage `s`. `SCRIPT_BITS[s]` is the smallest nonnegative integer `b` for which `N(s) * 2^b >= 2^160`. The resulting script-hash mean search work is never less than the address mean and is less than twice as large, under uniform-hash models. The listed script bits are normative and the counted `N(s)` values are supplied in the machine-readable schedule. They are not inferred by treating Base58 characters as equiprobable.

The first 17 contractions refine the address to `1Chi` and the TXID to `999`; subsequent stages add full characters. Some address contractions leave the TXID rule unchanged. The terminal script-hash rule is 75 leading bits toward repeated `7`: eighteen whole `7` digits followed by `6` or `7`, then any remaining valid hex digits. Address-space matching does not imply identical CPU cost for key derivation, script construction and valid-transaction generation.

### 3. Exact calendar and volume eligibility

`V(B)` counts all non-coinbase transactions in accepted blocks `H0+1` through `B`, inclusive; `V(H0)=0`. It counts neither retained records nor outputs. Coinbase transactions remain eligible for queries and admission but do not increment this counter. Integers MUST not overflow 32-bit counters or lose precision. Providers MUST disclose the provenance and verification/trust assumptions of their counts. Headers alone do not establish the cumulative transaction count; using a count service is a dependency, not a claim of header-only verification.

Set `G = T0 + 6 calendar months` and `E = T0 + 78 calendar months`, both added directly from `T0` in UTC, preserving time of day and clamping to the target month's last day. Define:

```text
w(i) = 11^(i-1) * 10^(26-i), for i = 1..26
W    = sum(w(i), i=1..26)
D    = E-G, in integer seconds
T(1) = G
T(s) = G + ceil(D * sum(w(i), i=1..s-1) / W), for s=2..27
candidate(B) = highest s with MTP(B) >= T(s) AND V(B) >= VOLUME[s],
               or 0 if none qualifies
```

The 26 post-runway intervals grow by approximately 10% after whole-second rounding. These are eligibility intervals, not empirical chain-growth estimates or guaranteed dwell times. Both gates must pass in the same accepted view. Insufficient volume can delay any stage indefinitely. A candidate is not an active stage: section 4 adds a fresh notice period to each actual contraction.

### 4. Deterministic notice rounds and following-block activation

Notices are part of the common algorithm. They MUST NOT be local timers that give providers different historical stages at the same accepted tip. A **notice round** freezes one target stage, its accepted triggering block, and a deadline exactly `1,209,600` MTP seconds (14 UTC days) after that block's MTP. This specification measures the algorithm's notice period using accepted-chain median time, not a provider's wall clock or block-count approximation. Public communications SHOULD start earlier; chain time is not a guarantee of exactly 14 elapsed wall-clock days for each recipient.

Initialize stage 0 with no pending round at `H0`. Process accepted blocks parent before child:

```text
stage(B)   = stage(B.parent)
pending(B) = pending(B.parent)

if pending(B) exists AND MTP(B.parent) >= pending(B).deadline
                     AND candidate(B.parent) >= pending(B).target:
  stage(B) = pending(B).target
  pending(B) = none

process transactions in B using stage(B)
compute V(B), MTP(B), and candidate(B)

if pending(B) is none AND candidate(B) > stage(B):
  pending(B) = {
    target: candidate(B),
    triggerHash: hash(B), triggerHeight: height(B),
    startedAt: MTP(B), deadline: MTP(B) + 1209600
  }
```

Every block uses one event stage, including for funding and spends. A notice trigger cannot apply a new rule to its own transactions. Even the block first reaching the notice deadline uses the old rule; its accepted child activates the frozen target. The event stage is `stage(B)`, not `candidate(B.parent)`.

Higher eligibility during a pending round MUST NOT silently change its target or shorten its warning. After activation, a higher candidate may start a new round in that same block, with its own fresh 14-day deadline. Direct jumps remain permitted: when a round starts with candidate 17 and active stage 0, it announces a direct contraction to 17. It need not activate the skipped intermediate stages. If target 5 was already frozen when candidate 27 becomes eligible, the service first activates 5, then gives a new round for 27. This prevents a newly eligible, more restrictive rule from borrowing an older target's notice.

The calendar table's 78-month value remains the final **eligibility** gate. With all volume thresholds met on time, the earliest final activation is no earlier than `E + 14 MTP days`, in the accepted child of a block reaching that notice deadline. Volume delays, a preceding pending round and block arrival can extend that. Actual contraction times may differ substantially from the nominal table intervals.

Each provider MUST publish a fresh stage-specific notice when a round starts, naming the old and target stages, filters, trigger, deadline, following-block rule and affected guarantees. A standing schedule announcement does not replace this notice. A jump crossing stage 13 MUST explicitly warn that complete confirmed-spend accounting ends. Providers MUST continuously publish the common schedule, current stage, frozen target and chain snapshot. A missed public notice is a conformance failure; it does not change the common historical stage. A provider unable to meet the notice obligation MUST preserve its existing customer commitments or identify a separate transitional service rather than inventing a different BRC stage.

An existing product entering this profile after the chain has advanced MUST separately announce its withdrawal at least 14 UTC calendar days before entry. It enters at the common chain stage and reconstructs admissions/spends using common historical stages; its entry announcement does not reset the algorithm. Operators SHOULD contact API users early, include response deprecation metadata, publish migration/export instructions and use request-distribution logs to assess awareness. Intentional latency changes or other business policies MUST be disclosed and cannot be used to claim an availability guarantee the operator is not delivering. Not every dormant user will see a notice; recovery consequences are described in section 8.

### 5. Accepted-chain authority and recovery retention

The currently accepted mainnet chain is the sole authority for `H0`, its MTP anchor, volume, notice rounds, stages, funding admission and spend-accounting effects. Rejected side branches have no effect. A reorganization MUST undo orphaned rounds and activations as well as transactions, then replay the replacement branch from a verified common ancestor. A replacement branch may lower the stage, remove a pending round, change which spend-accounting mode governed a transaction, or change funding admission. If `H0` changes, recompute all dependent state; if the chain no longer reaches it, the profile is inactive. The same accepted chain produces the same algorithmic stage and event-stage history for late joiners and existing providers.

Caches and durable checkpoints MUST bind hashes, ancestry, anchor, volume, active stage, pending round, query watermarks and ledger/watch state coherently. Heights, highest-ever stages and observed orphaned events cannot substitute for accepted ancestry. A restart MUST verify its checkpoint and replay/backfill any missing history before complete claims. Audit records about rejected branches may remain but MUST NOT affect current results.

Providers SHOULD retain enough durable recovery information for at least **100 accepted descendants** of the event that made it unnecessary for the active view. For retirement at accepted block `R` of height `H`, that recommendation protects it through `H+99`; `H+100` is the earliest removal consistent with that minimum, subject to longer needs. Use the effective activation block for contraction, the event block for a spend-state replacement, and the accepted entry snapshot for a late adopter withdrawing existing coverage. Record hashes and verify ancestry: orphaned blocks, side-branch heights, wall-clock time and cached highest-seen heights do not age the recommendation. If `R` is orphaned, re-establish any retirement decision from the replacement history.

The recommendation covers affected history records, previous output/admission/spend state, per-block undo material and coherent checkpoints. An equivalent compressed or recovery-only representation is sufficient. Operators MAY keep data indefinitely or choose a different retention policy based on their risk assessment; they MUST publish the chosen minimum, any departure from the recommended 100 blocks and its recovery implications. The analogy is the [100-block coinbase maturity constant](https://github.com/bitcoin-sv/bitcoin-sv/blob/master/src/consensus/consensus.h), not a proof of finality or an estimate of reorganization probability. This is neither a compulsory purge at 100 blocks nor a promise that deeper reorganizations cannot occur.

Data still needed for live promised coverage MUST remain available, including grandfathered output state. Providers MUST NOT destroy the only usable data needed by an in-progress recovery while continuing to claim that recovery is supported. They are responsible for their own retained data or verified reconstruction arrangements. If a reorganization broadens coverage, reconstruct **all** newly eligible history, including records predating the fork or `H0`, and restore the spend mode required by each historical block. A rollback from 13 to 12 can require backfilling spends previously outside the filtered feed. Until required state is verified, expose `rebuild_pending` and return unavailable rather than a complete miss, zero balance or false UTXO claim. No recovery duration or availability SLA is imposed here.

### 6. Free query coverage and commercial extensions

For a generalized funding/history record in the current free view:

```text
txidEligible(tx, s) = txidMatches(tx.txid, s)

addressRecordEligible(address, tx, vout, s) =
  tx.outputs[vout] pays exactly the canonical P2PKH script for address
  AND addressMatches(address, s) AND txidMatches(tx.txid, s)

scriptRecordEligible(scriptKey, tx, vout, s) =
  HASH160(tx.outputs[vout].lockingScript) == scriptKey
  AND scriptHashMatches(scriptKey, s) AND txidMatches(tx.txid, s)
```

TXID lookup covers every qualifying accepted-chain transaction from genesis through the declared indexed-through block. Address/script-hash history covers qualifying funding/output records over that same range. Here history does not mean a complete list of all incoming and outgoing activity. Core free queries MUST NOT silently omit qualifying older records or substitute a shorter time window. Missing ingestion or recovery data requires unavailable/explicitly incomplete coverage. A valid hit's transaction identifier and claimed accepted confirmation must be verified. A genuine miss is only absence from the complete declared scope and snapshot.

At activation, the guaranteed free keyspace contracts immediately. A request outside it MUST receive an explicit outside-free-coverage result or an expressly identified broader offer; a paywall or redirect MUST NOT be disguised as a lookup miss. Broader paid or complimentary responses MUST identify their own filters, pricing/entitlement and completeness/observation contract. This BRC's minimum guarantees do not automatically extend to that broader product. Physical retention or serving extra data does not make excluded data part of the guaranteed free scope.

Providers MAY retain a full index, share one backing database across products, or retain several broader stages. This profile contains no physical/logical deletion, data-layout or separate-database requirement. The economic objective is sustainable availability expectations, not compulsory destruction. Optional output and balance responses use section 7, whose grandfathering can retain funding references excluded from current generalized history/TXID lookup.

### 7. Output lists and balances: two spend-accounting modes

#### 7.1. Admission, grandfathering and key scope

An output is admitted to a key family's ledger when its funding transaction and that family's query key pass the event stage of the funding block. For earlier pre-activation history use stage 0. Evaluate historical admission at that block's event stage, not at the stage when a provider later discovers it. Admission for an address does not by itself admit the output to a script-hash ledger, or vice versa. Ignored nonqualifying deposits add no covered value and do not harm other admitted outputs.

Admitted output metadata MUST survive later funding-TXID contractions while needed for that ledger: keep the outpoint, amount, exact script/key relation, funding confirmation/admission stage and applicable spend state. The funding transaction may leave current generalized free lookup without removing this grandfathered state. An output-list response MAY identify its historical funding TXID even when generalized TXID lookup is outside the free keyspace. Current filtered history can consequently be empty while the ledger has positive value.

The query key itself is not grandfathered. If its address or script hash fails the current key rule, the free ledger promise ends; return outside-free-coverage, not zero. Active indexing/watch obligations for that key may stop, subject to the published recovery policy. A reorganization restoring the key requires verified historical reconstruction before its ledger guarantee resumes. There is no permanent disqualification for a nonqualifying spend, no poison-address rule, and no need to reset a key after a new qualifying deposit.

#### 7.2. Stages 0–12: complete confirmed-spend accounting

Providers offering output or balance operations MUST detect and account for every accepted confirmed spend of admitted outputs, irrespective of the spending TXID. They may use complete outpoint notifications rather than store every spending body, but a qualifying-TXID feed alone is insufficient for these stages. A nonqualifying spend simply debits the spent admitted output; it does not invalidate the key's remaining outputs or later deposits. Spending an ignored deposit does not debit the admitted ledger.

After complete admission and spend accounting through a common accepted snapshot, returned unspent admitted outputs may be described as **covered UTXOs**, and their integer-satoshi sum as a **covered balance**. This is still not the total balance of all deposits to the key: ignored deposits are excluded. Coinbase maturity and other spending restrictions remain distinct from unspent status and MAY be reported separately.

#### 7.3. Stages 13–27: qualifying-TXID spend accounting only

From stage 13 onward, an indexer MUST continue observing the complete qualifying-TXID stream and reflecting every accepted confirmed spend it contains of an admitted output, including grandfathered outputs. The spender's TXID is tested against the **event stage of its own accepted confirmation block**. A historically qualifying spend cannot be ignored retroactively because a later stage tightens the rule. A later spend of an old admitted output uses the later spend block's rule. All valid transactions within one block use that block's one event stage, with UTXO dependencies processed in valid transaction order.

For a transaction failing that event-stage TXID rule, the provider has no obligation to discover the spend or reflect it. It may therefore retain an output that has in fact already been spent and include its amount in the returned filtered ledger. It MUST NOT retain an output as unspent or count it in a returned amount if an accepted qualifying spend of that output has been established. An unconfirmed transaction is not a confirmed debit; expose mempool observations separately. A transaction merely naming an input without validated accepted-chain spend evidence is insufficient.

The required labels are **indexed TXOuts** and **filtered-spend balance**, with `spendCoverage = qualifying_txids_only` and `mayIncludeSpentOutputs = true`. The baseline service MUST NOT present those results as an unconditional accurate balance, a verified UTXO list or proof of spendability. If an API retains a historical `utxos` or `balance` field name, its binding MUST carry these explicit semantics on every response and document them for clients. Wrapping the same uncertain data in another endpoint name does not restore a UTXO guarantee.

To keep this ledger equal to the real unspent subset of its admitted funds, an owner MUST ensure that **every actual spending transaction**, including pre-signed, delegated or delayed transactions, qualifies under its accepted spend block's stage. Signing a transaction that qualifies only at broadcast is not enough. Owners cannot infer that the condition held merely from a positive filtered balance. Wallets SHOULD independently check spend status or use their own validated records before selecting outputs. They MAY target a narrower forthcoming keyspace and pay a fee more likely to obtain timely inclusion; neither a higher fee nor the following-block activation rule guarantees a confirmation time.

A provider MAY discover and apply additional validated nonqualifying spends, for example through a paid backing index or targeted status checks. It MUST identify that extended observation scope and its watermark; if it knows an output was spent, it MUST NOT label it a true UTXO. Extra observed debits can make two providers' post-stage-12 amounts differ without differing prefix rules. Clients MUST compare observation scope and snapshot as well as stage. The common free contract guarantees qualifying-spend accounting, not the absence of every unobserved spend.

#### 7.4. State transitions, truthful snapshots and reorganizations

Entering stage 13 changes the observation promise for all still-admitted outputs, including grandfathered ones. Providers may stop the whole-network spend watch after that activation while maintaining the qualifying stream. Debits correctly established during stages 0–12 MUST remain debited; the transition does not resurrect previously spent outputs. New qualifying funding can still be admitted, and ignored incoming payments still remain excluded.

| Accepted event | Required effect |
| --- | --- |
| Nonqualifying funding deposit | Do not admit it; other admitted funds are unaffected. |
| Later TXID rule excludes an admitted funding TXID | Preserve grandfathered ledger state while the key remains eligible. |
| Any confirmed spend of an admitted output during stages 0–12 | Debit that output, regardless of spender TXID. |
| Confirmed qualifying spend during stages 13–27 | Debit every consumed admitted output; do not return it as a UTXO or count its value. |
| Confirmed nonqualifying spend during stages 13–27 | May go unobserved/unreflected; disclose potential stale outputs and amounts. |
| Spend of only ignored funds | No debit to the admitted ledger. |
| Mixed admitted/ignored inputs | Apply the spend rule separately to each admitted input; do not retire the key. |
| Reorganization removes a funding or accounted spend | Undo it and replay admission/debits using replacement-block stages. |
| Reorganization restores a stage below 13 | Re-establish complete confirmed-spend accounting; unavailable until backfill completes. |

A ledger snapshot MUST lie on the accepted chain and be complete for the admission and spend-observation scope it claims. A lost qualifying-stream interval after stage 13 is a real coverage gap, not permission to ignore required spends; backfill it before complete filtered-ledger claims. Likewise, a stages-0–12 watch gap forbids a verified covered balance. Providers MAY return older verified as-of snapshots, clearly identified, but MUST NOT call them current unless their required observations reach the current accepted tip. Missing data is unavailable, never a fabricated zero. Stage-13 intentional exclusion is a defined observation limit, not an ingestion failure, and must be labeled differently.

### 8. Migration, recovery and services still required

#### 8.1. End-to-end transaction and recovery path

The [Bitcoin white paper](https://bitcoin.org/bitcoin.pdf), particularly sections 7 and 8, describes storage reclamation and verification with headers and Merkle branches, including dependence on honest network behavior and the need to handle invalidity alerts. The author treats SPV and direct exchange as the intended scalable end-user configuration. This BRC does not claim that inclusion proves current unspentness, that archival data becomes unnecessary to everyone, or that every business must run the same wallet implementation.

An implementer can build the following path from open specifications and source. These are distinct responsibilities, each of which needs operating resources:

1. **Construct and deliver the relevant transaction.** Use [BRC-29](../payments/0029.md) and [BRC-42](../key-derivation/0042.md) where applicable. A BRC-100 sender constructs with `createAction`, preserves the transaction and remittance/derivation metadata, and delivers them to the recipient or its chosen durable inbox. A receiving wallet uses `internalizeAction` to validate and retain its relevant outputs. An offline recipient needs a retained delivery route and retry/reconciliation policy, not a hope that a future address scan will find everything.
2. **Carry and validate evidence.** [BRC-62](../transactions/0062.md) BEEF, [BRC-95](../transactions/0095.md) Atomic BEEF and [BRC-67](../transactions/0067.md) describe transaction/ancestry/SPV handling. Preserve the bytes and required parents/proofs; verify identifiers, scripts, amounts and Merkle paths against the accepted header chain. Missing evidence must be obtained from the counterparty, retained store or an explicitly supported retrieval service. BEEF is not a promise that an arbitrary missing body can be recovered for free.
3. **Broadcast and track the submitted transaction.** Arcade's [implemented Teranode integration](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/arcade/blob/e48baded86b3ea8c934f0509ba2cd3927b765376/doc.md) submits transactions, follows network lifecycle events, registers tracked transactions for proof delivery and exposes status/callback interfaces. Submission acknowledgement is not confirmation or finality. Applications must reconcile later acceptance, rejection, conflicts, proofs and reorganization state.
4. **Acquire and preserve proofs.** Arcade's [current API contract](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/arcade/blob/e48baded86b3ea8c934f0509ba2cd3927b765376/openapi/arcade.openapi.yaml) carries mined Merkle paths and chain/reorganization interfaces. Proof delivery is tied to tracked transactions and configured upstream Merkle Service availability. The Toolbox [Arcade provider implementation](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/ts-stack/blob/9262cac6609ffa86145f7679a00b8d012e1cb6c7/packages/wallet/wallet-toolbox/src/services/providers/Arcade.ts) validates returned paths against its chaintracker and falls through when tracked proof data is absent. A lifecycle store keyed by transactions it was asked to handle is distinct from a promise of universal historical TXID retrieval. Preserve received evidence so future arbitrary historical proof requests are unnecessary for ordinary recovery.
5. **Track headers and accepted-chain changes.** [ChainTracks](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/ts-stack/blob/9262cac6609ffa86145f7679a00b8d012e1cb6c7/docs/infrastructure/chaintracks-server.md) supplies bulk/live headers, validation and tip/reorganization interfaces. Header integrity, work, linkage, source trust and synchronization still require implementation. On a reorganization, invalidate orphaned confirmations/proofs, refresh evidence and reconcile transaction/output state. A stale header source cannot establish a current view merely because an old inclusion proof still hashes correctly.
6. **Check and reconcile spend/conflict state.** Known-outpoint UTXO/spend checks, broadcast conflict handling, double-spend detection/deconfliction and relevant notifications remain necessary. They are scoped to transactions and outputs the parties care about, not necessarily an all-address discovery database. Inclusion and token-lineage proofs do not establish current UTXO membership. Treat a missing response, lag or inconclusive rejection as unknown; do not free reservations or promise spendability without appropriate evidence.
7. **Replicate and export wallet data independently of keys.** [BRC-38](../outpoints/0038.md) defines portable per-user records, [BRC-39](../outpoints/0039.md) protects the file, and [BRC-40](../outpoints/0040.md) defines incremental storage synchronization. Toolbox [storage code](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/ts-stack/blob/9262cac6609ffa86145f7679a00b8d012e1cb6c7/packages/wallet/wallet-toolbox/src/storage/WalletStorageManager.ts) provides `syncFromReader` and `updateBackups`. Use complete, verified synchronization and consistent versioned backups. Replication can replicate deletion or corruption; it is not by itself an independent retained backup.
8. **Restore with both authority and records.** The [wallet recovery guide](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/ts-stack/blob/9262cac6609ffa86145f7679a00b8d012e1cb6c7/docs/guides/wallet-backup-recovery.md) separates root-key recovery, transaction/derivation records, product configuration and backup access. The [portability implementation guide](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/ts-stack/blob/9262cac6609ffa86145f7679a00b8d012e1cb6c7/docs/guides/wallet-data-portability.md) identifies real export/import helpers and limits, including consistency and provider compatibility. Restore into an independently controlled compatible store, reconcile proofs and spend state, and rehearse loss of the original device and provider. Neither a seed nor a BRC-39 file alone supplies both halves.

Purpose-built overlays receive transactions for their topics rather than discovering every payment on chain. The open [Overlay Engine](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/ts-stack/blob/9262cac6609ffa86145f7679a00b8d012e1cb6c7/docs/packages/overlays/overlay.md) accepts submitted BEEF, applies topic admission and lookup rules and supports [BRC-76](../transactions/0076.md) graph-aware synchronization; [BRC-88](../overlays/0088.md) describes synchronization architecture. Operators still preserve relevant history, propagate spends/transitions and run their chosen storage and recovery processes. An overlay's topical index is not the universal free script-hash index this profile contracts.

Token protocols can require additional evidence: [BRC-176](../tokens/0176.md) preserves token-specific parent bodies back to the applicable deploy/authority for BSV-21/1Color lineage. Ordinary Atomic BEEF's SPV-minimal omission rules are insufficient for that packet. Parties must retain the evidence required by the token protocol as well as ordinary Bitcoin validation data; a token-validity packet does not prove unspent status or global supply.

#### 8.2. Implemented, deployed and planned capabilities

As of the research date, the pinned Toolbox, SDK, Overlay Engine, ChainTracks and Arcade sources implement these building blocks. A public [July 2026 Storage–Arcade–Monitor integration report](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/ts-stack/pull/244) records a bounded mainnet run in which 29 transactions mined, received proofs and reached completed wallet-storage state, and also records later failed load attempts and corrective work. It establishes an exercised deployed path, not a throughput ceiling, universal wallet compatibility, successful recovery under every failure, or proof that all current dependencies have already migrated.

Current Toolbox [service defaults](https://github.com/bsv-blockchain/ts-stack/blob/9262cac6609ffa86145f7679a00b8d012e1cb6c7/packages/wallet/wallet-toolbox/src/services/Services.ts) still include explorer-backed raw transaction, script-hash history, proof and UTXO-status services. Configurability and open source do not erase those dependencies automatically. Deployments must inventory which operations rely on them, retain/deliver required records, and replace, self-operate or commercially fund necessary retrieval/status services before removing a fallback they still need. No particular vendor or endpoint is mandated by this BRC, and no present public endpoint is promised to remain free.

[BRC-83](../transactions/0083.md) describes hierarchical transaction/graph aggregation and downstream proof completion. [BRC-80](./0080.md) describes version/TXID multicast subscription concepts. These are directions for further capacity and efficiency investment, not assertions that this exact profile's transport is already deployed. BRC-80's illustrative mapping carries 48 TXID bits; the final filter here needs 52, requiring broader-shard receipt and local filtering or a transport extension. All-version subscriptions, completeness, loss recovery and proof-distribution economics also need an actual binding. Responsible planning can use implemented direct-delivery systems now while investing in those improvements; it need not promise that every future component works perfectly today.

#### 8.3. Necessary roles and optional universal services

| Still needed for the selected use case | Responsibility/cost |
| --- | --- |
| Private keys, derivation/spending metadata and compatible signing logic | User/wallet custody and tested recovery; an index is not a key backup. |
| Relevant transaction and token ancestry, proof/broadcast state and durable delivery | Parties and chosen storage/inbox/overlay operators preserve and exchange it. |
| Header services, accepted-chain validation and reorganization alerting | Independently verified chain view with explicit source and availability assumptions. |
| Transaction broadcast, proof acquisition, known-outpoint spend checks and double-spend deconfliction | Transaction processors and scoped service providers under sustainable operating arrangements. |
| Storage synchronization, consistent backups and provider exit | Wallet/storage operators and users verify both data recovery and key recovery. |

The author does not consider universal free full-history TXID lookup, all-address/script-hash balance or UTXO enumeration, bulk historical block delivery, or arbitrary on-demand old Merkle proofs necessary prerequisites for every participant. Such products may remain valuable and may charge for retention, computation and delivery. This version contracts only the free index families it defines; it does not remove node validation, abolish archives, or set retirement rules for those other services. Future specifications may address their own free-availability expectations separately.

An owner returning with only a seed has no guaranteed free recovery outside the current keyspace, and post-stage-12 filtered results cannot establish actual unspentness even inside it. Existing wallet records, retained backups, counterparties or a willing historical/paid provider may enable reconstruction, without changing consensus ownership. This BRC cannot promise that a suitable provider exists, retains everything or will quote an affordable price. Operators should facilitate export/migration before withdrawal. The author accepts that missed migration can make recovery more costly or unavailable through the user's former service; the contract must state that consequence rather than imply that coins were invalidated or that keys alone solve it.

### 9. Logical responses, status and conformance

This is a service-profile contract, not a new HTTP, payment, authentication, provider-discovery or multicast wire protocol. Each implementation MUST document a binding preserving the following meanings:

| Logical state | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `found` | Verified record within the declared key, filter, observation scope and accepted snapshot. |
| `not_found` | No result in the complete declared scope/snapshot; not a claim of global nonexistence. |
| `outside_free_coverage` | Valid query outside the guaranteed free keyspace; any broader offer is identified separately. |
| `covered` | Stages 0–12: verified unspent admitted outputs/covered amount through a complete confirmed-spend snapshot. |
| `filtered` | Stages 13–27: complete for declared qualifying-spend observations, potentially including genuinely spent outputs. |
| `unavailable` | Required data, verification, continuity or recovery is incomplete; not a miss or zero. |
| `invalid_request` | Malformed encoding, invalid checksum/length or incompatible parameter encoding. |
| `unsupported` | Operation/family not offered or required native-key mapping unavailable. |
| `coverage_changed` | A pinned paginated view no longer matches the accepted chain/stage; restart. |

Every response MUST identify profile `234`, version `1`, operation/family, logical state, free versus separately offered entitlement, active stage, accepted snapshot hash/height and applicable completeness/watermarks. Valid non-mainnet/non-P2PKH addresses are outside this address family's scope, not malformed encodings. Output/balance responses MUST additionally identify the key family, amount in integer satoshis where returned, admission and spend-observation snapshots, `spendCoverage`, `mayIncludeSpentOutputs` and any extra observation scope. Metadata omission cannot turn a filtered ledger into a UTXO guarantee.

Status MUST expose the anchor hash/height/MTP, calendar and volume provenance, current and candidate stages, current key rules and bit counts, any frozen target/trigger/deadline, effective activation identity, offered features, ingestion/rebuild readiness, notice publication and the configured recovery policy/departures. Unknown fields must be explicit null plus reason, never fabricated zero/hash/stage. Hashes use canonical lowercase fixed-length hex; stage is an integer `0..27`. Exact counts, heights, amounts and weights MUST retain integer precision; JSON bindings MUST use decimal strings for quantities that can exceed safe JavaScript integer precision.

Pagination MUST pin stage, observation scope and accepted indexed-through hash. A reorganization or scope change invalidating that view requires `coverage_changed`. Never silently combine pages or serve an excluded key under an obsolete free guarantee. Separately offered broader views need their own contract and pinned metadata.

An adopting provider promises the required free scope and truthful semantics for the service it actually offers. It MUST NOT advertise live/complete conformance while silently operating a known incomplete index or permanently replacing every supported operation with unavailable. Temporary unavailable responses are required during real gaps; this BRC sets no universal SLA and does not compel eternal service. Providers may close or change products with clear notice and must cease claiming a guarantee they no longer deliver.

## Implementations and conformance evidence

No implementation of **this contraction profile**, measured saving or provider adoption is asserted by describing existing migration infrastructure. Reference code SHOULD calculate exact gates, notice rounds, all three predicates, admitted ledgers and spend modes, with coherent accepted-chain rollback and truthful responses.

The [machine-readable schedule](./media/0234/schedule.json) fixes stages, exact volume/bit values, address-space counts and calendar weights. The [conformance vectors](./media/0234/conformance-vectors.json) exercise encoding boundaries, script HASH160 mapping, calendar clamping, notice/deadline/following-block behavior, frozen targets, direct jumps, rejected/orphaned triggers, grandfathering, both spend modes and their reorganization boundary. Synthetic decoded effects are not signed mainnet transactions or block proofs; raw-hash predicate fixtures do not assert known spendable script preimages.

Run the [standalone vector verifier](./media/0234/verify_vectors.py) with `python3 opinions/media/0234/verify_vectors.py` from the repository root. It checks the mathematical tables and synthetic examples using standard-library code with RIPEMD160 support; it does not contact the network or certify a deployed indexer.

An implementation's evidence MUST include same-tip stage agreement across independent starts, undo/replay of notice and ledger state, complete historical reconstruction when coverage expands, all-spend accounting through stage 12, qualifying-spend debits from stage 13, truthful stale-output labeling, recovery gaps and the configured retention policy. Scalar stage calculation alone is not evidence of index-service conformance. Operators SHOULD exercise migration, exports, loss of a provider and restoration before withdrawing coverage.
